HOOK FORMULA
Opening: Dated historical scene plus unusual physical action; no greeting or definition.
Promise: How does a small discovery become world-changing technology?
Payoff: Answer through successive problems, proof, manufacturing and impact; resolve by final 5%.
Refs: “In 1887, a young physics teacher”; “his homemade crossbow.”

SPOKEN VOICE
Average: ~16–18 words; fragments/full sentences ~1:5 (estimated; punctuation noisy).
Markers/min: now 0.6; so 0.4; well 0.35; really 0.45; actually 0.16.
Intensifiers: really ~0.45/min; actually ~0.16/min; incredibly 0.
Repetition/restarts: ~8–10 events; triples actions for emphasis, with self-correction.
Contractions: mostly.
I/we/you: ~7:7:10; “you” mainly teaches through hypotheticals.
Spoken refs: “That is cool” — spontaneous live reaction; “That's crazy” — casual surprise marker; “That couldn't be right” — immediate doubt expressed; “I would've thought” — prior expectation exposed; “I didn't expect it” — honest surprise admitted.

WORD-CHOICE HABITS
Says “homemade crossbow,” not “device,” because concrete visual.
Says “barely hanging on,” not “loosely attached,” because adds tension.
Says “wild goose chase,” not “unlikely project,” because familiar metaphor.
Says “leaky truck,” not “signal attenuation,” because physical analogy.
Says “Whoopie,” not “successful,” because human spontaneity.

PERSONALITY MOVES
Prior expectations: ~2 personal admissions; states assumptions before demonstrations.
Admits wrong: no factual retraction; ~2 expectation corrections or surprises.
Uncertainty: ~8 knowledge gaps/questions across the investigation.

HUMOR AND FACT DELIVERY
~15–20 light moments: situational interruption, dry anti-climax, absurd scale, cultural comparison, live-demo reactions. Facts: comparisons/analogies, then measurements; “mass of the observable universe.”

STRUCTURE MAP
Historical hook 6% > old-system problem 9% > alternative/obstacle 9% > scientific mechanism 22% > sponsor 4% > proof 4% > manufacturing 23% > digital impact 4% > modern factory 13% > reflection/CTA 6%.
Re-hooks: ~20–25 (~0.6/min); solve one obstacle, expose the next question or failure.

ENDING AND CTA
Ends with philosophical reflection, then team appreciation and resource invitation; no callback or cliffhanger.
CTA: requests comments for a follow-up at 32:48 (soft, casual); promotes newsletter/Q&A at 34:14 (casual).
Last sentence: resource pointer — “you can find her Q&A right there.”

RULES FOR THE SCRIPT AI
1. Open with one concrete event before defining the subject. (high)
2. Answer the hook question by the final 5% of runtime. (high)
3. Explain each mechanism in demonstration, principle, application order. (high)
4. Translate at least 3 abstract quantities into physical comparisons. (high)
5. After each solution, introduce another obstacle at least 3 times. (high)
6. Show one failed test or uncertain measurement before claiming success. (high)
7. Alternate technical explanations with short human reactions at least 4 times. (medium)
8. End with one broader takeaway after the technical payoff. (high)

DO NOT COPY
Crossbow, laser and factory demonstrations require specialist access, equipment and safety controls.
The 81-second travel-eSIM sponsorship is presenter-specific and interrupts the proof sequence.
